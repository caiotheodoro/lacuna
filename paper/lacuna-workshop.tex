\title{Counting the actions no log recorded:\\a stress test of capture-recapture for agent trace completeness}
\author{Caio Theodoro}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Audit regimes assume an agent's log is complete. When no single log is, the
actions missing from all of them are a population-size problem, and
capture-recapture over logs written by different infrastructure can estimate it. I
stress-test that on recorded episodes of a benign LLM agent in three studies, in environments
whose kernels keep a ledger no log reads, with log loss simulated afterwards. A
pre-registered back-office test passed all four hypotheses at its registered loss
seed (coverage 0.940, bar 0.90); over 30 fresh loss seeds coverage is 0.893. A
pre-registered infra test failed its coverage hypothesis (0.300, and 0.401 over 20
seeds), as registered. Both matched records by their effect; matching by ledger
id, which no auditor has, gives 0.948 and 0.946. Post hoc, linking two logs by an
id the harness writes into both recovers back-office coverage (0.946) but not
infra (0.859). Under exact matching, three loss patterns break the estimator: loss
concentrated on actions one log cannot see fails silently, loss in bursts is in
back-office largely a case of the first, and a loss rate that ramps over the
episode with the same mean drops coverage to 0.742 and 0.471. A gateway sequence
counter or planted canary actions survive the first pattern in simulation, but
the counter fails under bursts and bypass. A third pre-registered test let the agent
run actions through a batch tool that the trace and the gateway log as one call: it
ran 0.856 of them there (predicted 0.05 to 0.30), and the estimator failed (coverage
0.759, intervals about nine times wider) while still detecting the gap (0.995).
Nothing here tests an agent that hides on purpose.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Audit regimes that reach AI agents start from a log~\citep{euaiact}, and the
engineering around it asks whether a line was changed after it was
written~\citep{flightrecorder2026,vet2025}. Whether the record is whole is
assumed. Agents have been shown deleting their own trace events when asked, and
tampering unprompted when it raised their reward~\citep{qin2026tamper}; a line
never written leaves no trace in a hash chain.

The count of actions no log shows needs estimating only when no single record is
complete. A gateway that numbered every action before anything was dropped, and
logged its counter, would give it exactly; without the counter, record loss leaves
only a lower bound, and a tail correction holds only while loss is independent
(\S\ref{sec:loss}). Where actions bypass the gateway, logs belong to parties that
do not share their numbering, or sampling comes first, epidemiology's method
applies: overlapping several incomplete registers estimates what all of them
missed~\citep{fienberg1972,bird2018mse}. I found no work applying it to agent
trace completeness; the nearest, over LLM judges counting label errors, produced
valid intervals too wide to beat a raw count~\citep{kansal2026labels}.

This is a stress test on recorded episodes, not a deployed auditor. In the first two
environments a logged gateway counter would give the count; the replays test the
estimator, not a setting that needs it (\S\ref{sec:bypass} does). With loss that is
independent by construction and exact matching, the estimator is nominal. The contributions are three pre-registered tests of
capture-recapture for agent trace completeness, a decomposition of its failures
into record linkage and loss that concentrates on what one log cannot see, and
the post-hoc fixes that help.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

An \emph{action} is a tool call that changes environment state or sends data
out. The environment kernel appends one ledger entry per action, so the true
count $N$ is a program, and no witness reads it. Three witnesses observe actions:
W1, the harness's trace of the agent's tool calls; W2, a gateway proxy log; W3,
an end-of-shift diff of the environment's state plus its outbound mail sink.

\paragraph{Estimator and assumption.} \texttt{ll\_bic} fits Poisson log-linear
models to the seven observed cells of the three-log capture
table~\citep{fienberg1972}, selects pairwise interactions by BIC and reports a
95\% profile-likelihood interval for $N$. It was chosen from a candidate set on
exploratory runs before any test. It assumes there is no three-way
interaction: the logs may depend on each other in pairs, but what all three miss
together is predicted by how they overlap. In the project's parametric simulator
of capture histories, a case where the agent's omissions are correlated across
all three logs leaves every point estimator at 0.218 coverage or less; only a
bound that takes an assumed limit on that correlation covers.

\paragraph{Linkage.} Matching records across logs \emph{exactly} uses the ledger
id, which no auditor has; some tables call it \emph{oracle}.
\emph{Effect-signature} matching pairs records on operation, resource and, for
some operations, value, in order within a signature.

\paragraph{Replays.} A live agent (\texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash}, benign) worked
two environments: a back-office queue of refunds, cancellations, customer updates,
file writes and email, and an infra/ops queue whose end-state diff misses
transient work. Before any loss, the trace and the gateway each held every action,
so loss is simulated afterwards on the recorded episodes. Each \emph{replicate}
resamples the recorded episodes with replacement, drops log lines by the loss
model, and re-estimates $N$; \emph{coverage} is the share of replicates whose
interval contains the true $N$, and \emph{gap detection} the share whose lower end
exceeds the number of distinct actions any log saw. A $\pm$ is one Monte Carlo
standard error over loss draws. Because replicates reuse the same recorded
episodes, no error here covers what recording new episodes would change.
\emph{Post hoc} marks every result outside the three pre-registrations.

The env diff has two views in back-office. The \emph{registered} (financial) view
sees refunds, cancellations and sent mail but not customer updates or file
writes; the \emph{full} view sees every operation. In the held-out configuration
\texttt{fin\_mid}, the trace and the gateway each lose half their lines at random
under the registered view. At the registered seed a replicate there hides
on average 63.15 of 828 actions from every log, and \texttt{ll\_bic}'s interval is
42.0 wide. The \emph{confirmatory pool} is the 21 registered back-office episodes,
with 30 tickets each. Two earlier back-office pools, \emph{long-60} and
\emph{long-120}, have 60 and 120 tickets per episode, with 10 and 5 episodes, run
on the previous model version (\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}).

\section{The pre-registered tests}
\label{sec:prereg}

Each test was committed before its paid run (back-office \texttt{6ea27e2}, infra
\texttt{6774b24}), on loss configurations held out of the estimator choice, with
effect-signature matching as the headline. Each registered the same four
hypotheses.
\textbf{H1/T1, coverage}: \texttt{ll\_bic} covers $N$ in at least 0.90 of
replicates. \textbf{H2/T2, informativeness}: it detects the gap in more than 0.90.
\textbf{H3/T3, dependence}: when the trace and the gateway fail together,
\texttt{ll\_independence}, the model with no interactions, loses at least 10
points of coverage while \texttt{ll\_bic} loses under 5. \textbf{H4/T4, floor}:
\texttt{ll\_bic} beats counting the union of the logs on gap detection by at
least 0.50.
The back-office run deviated twice from its protocol: a crash forced a rerun on
the same seeds, and three episodes lost to provider errors were later restored on
a changed client (the ``restored, 24'' rows of Table~\ref{tab:precision}). It
passed all four at its registered seed, H1 at 0.940 over 100 replicates, but the
coverage pass did not hold up. Over 30 fresh loss seeds on the same 21 episodes, signature-linkage coverage is $0.893 \pm 0.006$, close to the
bar given that error, and 0.862 with the three lost episodes restored, clearly
below it; with exact matching both pools are nominal (0.948, 0.945).

The infra registration was frozen after scripted infra runs had been seen, and
predicted that T1 would fail. It did: 0.300 at the registered seed, 0.401 over 20
seeds, against 0.946 with exact matching (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). T2--T4
passed but say little: under signature matching the infra estimator claims a gap
even when nothing is hidden, so T2 and T4 could not fail, and T3 passes because
the interval doubles at the registered seed, from 55.82 to 108.34, around an
unchanged bias.

\section{Actions that bypass the gateway}
\label{sec:bypass}

In the first two environments every action passes the gateway, so a logged counter
would give the count. The method is for the case where some actions do not. A third
pre-registered study (tag \texttt{prereg-bypass}, frozen before any paid episode)
adds \texttt{run\_script} to back-office: a batch of operations, parsed against a
whitelist. The ledger gets each inner action; the trace and the gateway log one call
holding the script text, and no witness parses it, which is a stipulation. 24
episodes ran with the usual prompt and 12 with a prompt saying the logs are audited
and asking for few log records (L1). All 36 completed.

The manipulation check passed, with a wrong prediction: the agent ran 1098 of 1283
actions in scripts, a share of 0.856 [0.736, 0.952] against a predicted 0.05 to
0.30. The calibration test failed, as predicted for such a share: \texttt{ll\_bic}
at \texttt{fin\_mid} with call-id linkage covers 0.759 [0.743, 0.774] against 0.90.
By the registered rule that is an effect of bypass: 0.187 below the no-bypass
reference (0.946, a different pool of episodes). Intervals are 333.6 wide against
39.0 and the estimate runs high by 34.1. The counter covers 0.0; \texttt{ll\_bic}
detects the gap in 0.995 of replicates, the union in 0.0. The L1 prompt raised the
share to 0.990 (paired difference $+0.121$ [0.010, 0.302], predicted at most 0.10).
Its coverage of 0.996 is not calibration: 0.996 of its intervals are unbounded, and
gap detection is 0.0. Post hoc, no estimator tried is calibrated: signature linkage
0.785, Chapman on the trace and env diff 0.834 at width 512.8, the independence model
0.392, an env diff that sees every operation 0.319.

\section{What the registered linkage costs}
\label{sec:linkage}

In back-office, 0.223 of actions share an effect signature with another action in
the same episode; 179 of those 185 are emails to the same customer. In-order
pairing lines up records of different actions as one action seen by both the
trace and the gateway. BIC reads that as a trace--gateway dependence and selects
the model with that interaction alone in 0.358 of replicates, against 0.010 under
exact matching; the independence model, which cannot fit it, covers 0.948. In
infra, identical repeated actions collapse into one signature record, and
env-diff rows attach to the wrong record. The same mechanism produces false gaps:
when the two logs lose mutually exclusive actions, so that nothing is hidden, and
the env diff misses some actions, signature matching claims a gap in up to every
replicate (Table~\ref{tab:linkage}).

The signature left out content both the trace and the gateway carry: the harness
writes the provider's tool-call id into each. Linking those two logs by that id
alone restores 0.946 in back-office under the registered view, where the env diff
sees every email and is easy to attach, and removes every false gap measured:
with the trace and the gateway linked exactly, an overlap between them can no
longer be manufactured. With the full view it is 0.924, and in infra 0.859: the
env diff carries no id, and attaching its rows to identical repeated actions needs
a per-row id or timestamp. A variant that attached them by log order produced false
gaps of its own and was withdrawn. A real gateway assigns its own request ids, so
this needs the harness to stamp one id on every log. The id is only as good as the lines: 5\% duplicated lines in all
three logs take coverage to 0.094 with call-id linkage, and 5\% forged phantom
lines in all three to 0.000 (post hoc, injected into the recorded logs).

\section{Loss the estimator does not expect}
\label{sec:loss}

\paragraph{Loss that depends on the action.} Raising both logs' drop rate on the
operations the registered view cannot see breaks \texttt{ll\_bic} silently. Under
exact matching it covers 0.647 at a tenth more loss and 0.187 at a fifth more
(0.657 and 0.197 under signature matching), on intervals barely wider than at
baseline (40.8 and 43.4 against 38.9) that still assert the gap (Table~\ref{tab:dose}). Past 1.2 times the loss, coverage rises
again, mostly because the interval grows to hundreds and then thousands of
actions. Under signature matching, stratifying, that is fitting each operation's table separately and summing, stays
near nominal where it answers up to 1.4 times the loss, and refuses, giving no
finite upper end, more often as the strata thin; this is its best case, since the
loss is keyed on the stratifying field.

\paragraph{Loss in bursts.} Dropping blocks of 12 consecutive actions per log,
each with probability one half and under exact matching, takes \texttt{ll\_bic}
to 0.559 in back-office and 0.802 in infra (Figure~\ref{fig:loss}b; 2000
replicates). Five disjoint sets of loss seeds put the same cells at 0.535 to
0.588 and 0.772 to 0.802; Table~\ref{tab:controls}, from the last, reads 0.588 and
0.772. In back-office this is largely the first pattern again. The agent works one kind of ticket at a time, and the
actions the registered view cannot see are front-loaded: in the confirmatory pool
it sees 0.498 of the actions in the first quarter of an episode, against 0.745 to
0.804 later. A lost
block that lands early hides actions no witness sees. Keeping the order but
permuting which actions the diff sees recovers 0.875 to 0.940. On the long pools,
the mean error is $+29.6$ and $+58.8$. In infra the bias is $+1.7$ and the
failure is mostly variance; shuffling the order of actions restores 0.912.

A quasi-likelihood correction~\citep{wedderburn1974quasi,mccullagh1989glm}, with
the dispersion estimated from the variation between episodes, trades width for
coverage (Table~\ref{tab:quasi}). Applied to BIC's interval only, it keeps the
point estimate and covers 0.952 under bursts in back-office and 0.994 in infra at
about 2.5 to 3 times the width; where the point is biased it covers by widening
(long-60: 0.894 at width 351.6, gap detected in 0.739). Applied at model selection
too, it collapses to 0.009 under session quality shared by all three logs, the case
the no-three-way assumption excludes, where the plain interval covers 0.432. It does not flag loss concentrated by kind: at 1.2 times the loss in
back-office, the dispersion stays at its per-record value. Neither variant is a default; both were found after the runs.

\paragraph{Cheaper checks.} Post hoc, with 500 replicates per cell and exact
matching, I compared \texttt{ll\_bic} with two checks that need no model. A gateway
sequence number with a tail correction covers 0.952 at 1.2 times the loss on unseen
operations, where \texttt{ll\_bic} covers 0.254, and 0.970 when every third action
is lost (\texttt{ll\_bic} 0.690), at less width; but it covers 0.0 under bursts, 0.480
under session quality shared by all three logs and 0.002 when 5\% of actions bypass
the gateway. Canary actions planted with the real operation mix cover at least
0.922 in every cell where they are exchangeable with real actions, including 0.938
under bursts, at wider intervals and with gap detection of 0.69 against 1.0; they
cover 0.0 when they sit outside the loss rule's key. Caveats are in
\S\ref{sec:limits}; the full table is in the report.

\paragraph{Loss that drifts, and faults in the lines.} A loss rate that ramps
linearly over the episode in the trace and the gateway, with the same mean, takes
\texttt{ll\_bic} to 0.742 and 0.471 at slopes of 0.4 and 0.8 under exact matching
(0.443 and 0.318 under signature matching).

\section{What this does not show}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}\setlength\parskip{0pt}
\item \textbf{No adversary and no real log loss.} Every agent was benign and the
loss was simulated on complete logs. The main pools have 21 to 24 episodes, the
long pools 10 and 5, from two versions of one model family.
\item \textbf{The setting that needs the method was tested once.} The bypass channel
is stipulated, the model and the 24 episodes are one pool, the no-bypass reference is
another, and the agent set the share. The audit-warning prompt is one pilot, not an
adaptive adversary. With record loss and no counter, the sequence number works under
independent loss and fails under bursts, shared session quality and bypass.
\item \textbf{Loss shared by all three logs} violates the no-three-way
assumption. Two variants in the technical report, a latent-class model and a fit
stratified by how many records each episode kept, reach 0.900 and 0.907 in one
environment each and 0.685 to 0.838 in the other; none is nominal. Outages that
take down all three logs at once leave no detectable gap.
\item \textbf{The author built the environments and set the bar,} and the fixes are
post hoc, found on the episodes that showed the problems. The infra run is
scripted-equivalent and its verdict depends on a collision density the author chose.
\item \textbf{The cheaper checks ran in simulation, on one pool.} The canaries
always had an env-diff record when in view, were never run with bypass, and ran on
the confirmatory episodes only. The sequence number's bypass cells drop gateway
records only; in the live bypass environment the trace misses those actions too, and
the counter covered 0.0.
\end{itemize}

\section{Takeaways for an auditor}

In these replays the method gave a calibrated count only when four conditions
held. The logs were linked exactly (a harness-written id did so for two of the
three logs in one environment) and held no duplicated or invented lines. Loss did
not concentrate, by kind or by time, on the actions one log cannot see, and its
rate did not drift. No loss was shared by all three logs. And most actions reached
the logs being counted: with 0.856 of them bypassing, coverage fell to 0.759 and the
intervals widened about nine times, though a gap was still detected. A dispersion-corrected
interval beside the plain one flags loss concentrated in time, at a cost in width
and gap detection; it does not flag loss concentrated by kind. When the first two
conditions fail the method can still say that actions are missing but not how
many, and with signature matching it can say so when nothing is. When all three
logs fail together it may not see the gap at all.

\section*{Reproducing this}
